🗺️ How we got here: the decision timeline

Every decision, change of direction, build, correction and hypothesis, oldest first. Nothing is deleted: a change of direction is a new entry.

  1. 2026-10-29 🧭 Decision
    Round 11 opens: an ad visitor's first win is the goal
    The paid-ad funnel is judged by one thing: does a stranger from an ad reach a first win (the welcome video and its Quick check) on the platform?
  2. 2026-11-09 11:25 🛠️ Built
    C118: Ali's 109-second avatar welcome video replaces the program video
    Self-hosted, first assignment for every Explorer who had not finished it (804 learners), with a Quick check rewritten from its transcript.
    PR
  3. 2026-11-09 11:25 🔬 Hypothesis
    The avatar video gets at least 20% of the people who press play to watch 75%
    Baseline with the old YouTube video: 7 of 51 brand-new sign-ups finished (14%). First 27.8 hours with the new one: 2 of 10 (20%), too few to call. 8 of the 9 who quit left within 18 seconds.
  4. 2026-11-10 07:03 📏 Measured
    C119: the player records exactly where a learner leaves the video
    Closing the tab now sends a final watch beat. Proven on production: a tab closed at 8.3 seconds recorded 8 seconds. Before this, a quit under 15 seconds left no trace.
    PR
  5. 2026-11-10 15:10 🔀 Change of direction
    The landing page now promotes the Open House; the account is created by the Open House sign-up
    Ali's call. Visitors save a seat at the live Open House, and that one form also creates their $0 account and sends a welcome email pointing at the video. Every number in this report starts here.
  6. 2026-11-10 15:10 🔬 Hypothesis
    Asking for an Open House seat converts at least 20% of landing page visitors
  7. 2026-11-11 05:40 📏 Measured
    C120: the Open House funnel tested end to end, with a rerunnable step report
    A test sign-up passed every step on production. The real numbers showed the leak: 5 sign-ups, 5 accounts, 5 welcome emails, 0 people entered. The welcome email lands in Gmail's Updates tab.
    PR
  8. 2026-11-11 07:20 🛠️ Built
    C121: an Access your account button signs a new sign-up straight in
    Ali's idea: skip the trip to the inbox. The email stays as the way back. An address that already has an account is told to sign in.
    PR
  9. 2026-11-11 07:20 🔬 Hypothesis
    With the button, at least 70% of brand-new accounts go in
    Before the button: 0 of 5. Note that this week's window still includes sign-ups from before 7:20 AM on 11 Nov, who never saw the button.
  10. 2026-11-11 12:20 📏 Measured
    C123: a weekly report follows every person through every fork
    First edition: 53 visitors, 14 sign-ups, 13 new accounts, 4 entered (all by the button), 0 pressed play. Facebook converted 40.7% of its visitors, Instagram 9.1%.
    PR
  11. 2026-11-11 13:00 ✏️ Correction
    "Finished the video" now means watched 75%, and collecting the credit is its own step
    C120 and the first C123 edition counted a finish only when the credit was collected. A learner who watched 97% and never clicked showed as not finishing. Both numbers are now shown.
  12. 2026-11-11 13:00 🔬 Hypothesis
    Once the page makes play the obvious next step on a phone, at least half of the people who reach the welcome video press play
    4 brand-new accounts, all on phones, opened the welcome video card within 4 seconds of signing up and sent no watch beat at all. A later phone learner played to 97% and was recorded, so phone beats work: these 4 most likely never pressed play.
  13. 2026-11-11 13:00 🧭 Decision
    C124: one starred move a week, emailed Wednesdays 8 AM CT to the marketing lead, the program lead and Ali
    The report now names the single move with the greatest impact and attaches a prompt that makes it. Every move made is added to this ledger with the hypothesis it tests, so the next report grades it.
  14. 2026-11-12 07:00 📏 Measured
    Collecting the credit runs at 88.4% across all 43 learners who earned it, not the 67% a three-person week suggested
    The 12 Nov edition starred "1 watched 75%+ and never collected the credit" and graded the step at 66.7% (2 of 3). Measured across every learner with progress on the welcome video: 43 have watched 75%+, 38 collected, 5 did not - 88.4% against the 90% target, so the step is close to target and the weekly figure was small-sample noise. Of the 5, four never signed in again after crossing the threshold, so they left mid-session rather than being refused; the credit stays collectable because watchVerdictFor reads the stored watch state and returns met=true whenever they return. Exactly one crossed 100% and signed in again 14 minutes later without collecting, which is the only real signal here and is n=1. Also worth saying plainly: 0 learners hold the credit with under 75% watched, so the gate is sound, and the insight wording "the platform did not count it" overstates a deliberate design - 75% unlocks the button and the click awards the points.

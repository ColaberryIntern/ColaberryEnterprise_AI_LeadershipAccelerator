How funding works

Every line of a 2,500 dollar scholarship.

A fully funded scholarship costs 2,500 dollars. Most of it buys training. The rest pays for running the program honestly. Here is the whole breakdown, so you do not have to take our word for the shape of it.

What 2,500 dollars buys

Allocation of one fully funded twelve month scholarship.
Where it goes Amount Share
Approved training provider, at 150 dollars a month for twelve months 1,800 72%
Scholarship administration 375 15%
Scholar support fund 200 8%
Payment fees and refund reserve 125 5%
Total 2,500 100%

These are planning allocations for the first ten scholarships. They are not final accounting categories, and we will revise them against what the program actually costs.

What the 375 dollars pays for

Administration is the least interesting line on the table and the one people are most right to ask about. It is not a management fee taken off the top. It pays for the work that makes a scholarship a program rather than a wire transfer.

  • Reviewing applications and making awards.
  • Checking on scholars during the year and stepping in when something slips.
  • Reporting back to partners and to the people who funded a scholarship.
  • The technology the program runs on.
  • Bookkeeping, compliance and audit support.
  • Verifying outcomes rather than asserting them.

When a scholarship is awarded

Only when the whole 2,500 dollars is raised and settled. We do not award a scholarship against money we expect to receive, because the person who would find out that the expectation was wrong is the scholar, nine months in.

Money already promised to a scholar is not available for a new one. The balance that matters is what is left after refunds, current scholars and unpaid provider bills are taken out. Each time that uncommitted balance reaches 2,500 dollars, one more scholarship can be awarded.

When the provider gets paid

Monthly, after the month, and never in advance. Before each 150 dollar payment we confirm that the scholar was enrolled, that the training was actually available, and that participation or approved leave was recorded. The provider invoices, we verify, an authorised person approves, and the payment is recorded with its evidence.

The written provider agreement and the board approval of that monthly rate are still to be completed. Until they are, no provider payment is made and no scholarship is awarded.

If a scholar pauses

Payments pause with them. The unspent commitment stays with Career Pathways Network, and a person decides whether the scholar resumes, transfers or the award closes.

If a scholar leaves

The provider keeps only the months it delivered. Someone who leaves after four months has cost 600 dollars, and the remaining 1,200 goes back into the scholarship program.

If more is raised than needed

It funds the next eligible scholar. It is not refunded to the provider and it is not held against a named individual.

If a provider changes

The scholarship is a year of approved training, not a year at one named school. We are not locked to any provider and no provider has an exclusive claim on these funds.

Who controls the money

Career Pathways Network does, at every point. That has three consequences worth stating plainly, because other models exist and people will reasonably assume one of them.

There are no personal fundraising accounts. A scholar's story may move you to give, and we will happily tell you that it did. What you gave is still a contribution to the scholarship program, not money belonging to that person, and we will never describe a total as theirs.

A partner pool is still a charitable pool. Money raised through a church, employer or community group supports eligible scholars connected to that community. Career Pathways Network still decides who is eligible, who is selected, and where money moves if it cannot be used as intended.

Giving does not buy influence over selection. Not for a donor, not for a partner organisation, and not for a provider.

Before you give

Career Pathways Network does not yet have federal tax exempt status. Contributions are not tax deductible at this time, and we will not tell you otherwise to make giving easier.

We are also not taking payments through this site yet. The support page captures your details and a person follows up.

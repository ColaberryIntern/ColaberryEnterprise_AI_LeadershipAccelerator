What we build

We build operating systems for work that currently lives in people’s heads.

Organised by the outcome you are buying, not by the technique underneath it. The technique is our problem.

Four outcomes

Reduce operational drag

The recurring manual work that never reaches a roadmap because it belongs to nobody and consumes everybody’s morning. Usually a spreadsheet, a shared mailbox, and one person who knows how it really works.

Make decisions visible

Most operational knowledge is undocumented not because people are careless, but because nothing ever asked them to write it down. We make the asking automatic.

Automate controlled work

Work repetitive and consequential enough that nobody trusts a script with it. The answer is not a better script; it is a system that records what it did and stops where a person is required.

Prove quality before release

Evidence recorded against each requirement, and a gate that can refuse. The interesting output is the refusal.

What that looks like in practice

A workflow becomes a contract
Before anything is built, the work is written down as requirements and acceptance criteria you sign off. If we cannot describe your workflow back to you accurately, we are not ready to build it.
Agents build against that contract
They work inside declared limits, on declared repositories, at a declared risk level. Higher risk means tighter limits and more human approval, and that mapping is enforced by the system rather than by good intentions.
Evidence accumulates as it goes
Tests, browser runs, security scans and accessibility checks are recorded against the requirement they prove. Not a report at the end — a record throughout.
You hold the gates
Designs, decisions and releases wait for a named person. Nothing ships because a build succeeded.

The December pilot, in detail

Testing with project managers rather than with jobs. The PM screen is the piece that replaces the red folder, and the PMs are the bottleneck Jamie named.

Why PMs and not jobs

Jamie's own description of the month is a paper relay: she prints a blank requisition, puts it in a red folder, walks it to the PM, the PM hand-writes what he wants to bill, Brian signs in blue, Nick signs in black, and it comes back to her. Her words on where the time goes once a PM finally sits down with it: "when they sit and they do them, it's like 15 minutes, but it's to sit there and do it. If it's on the screen, I think it would be a lot more utilized than it would be in that red folder on paper." Testing two jobs would prove the arithmetic. Testing two PMs proves the thing she is actually buying.

What the pilot covers

  • We load the pilot jobs ourselves. Their schedules of values come straight out of the workbooks we already read, so the upload screen is not on the critical path for December.
  • The PM enters this period and stored materials on screen, with last month's column already carried forward, which is the column Jamie retypes by hand today.
  • Jamie finalizes and exports the G702 and continuation sheets, and compares them against the same requisition built in Excel.
  • Brian and Nick stay on paper. The approval chain is Phase B. Nothing about the pilot depends on it.

The pilot moves Phase A's usable date in by about a week Rev 4 had Phase A finishing 11 December. The PMs need their requisitions around the 8th, so it now has to be usable by 5 December - three and a half weeks from the 12 November start rather than four and a half. What absorbs that is the schedule-of-values upload screen, which moves to the hardening window; for the pilot we load those jobs by hand. Everything the PM and Jamie touch stays in.

The one decision this schedule cannot make for itself

A full parallel run in January and the Phase B build both land on one builder in the same weeks.

Phase B is about six engineer-weeks: sequential approvals, the reminder calendar, electronic signature, the change-order gate and retainage. Rev 4 ran it 5 January to 13 February so that the product was live for the February billing run. A full-volume parallel run across every job and every PM in the first three weeks of January sits on top of that, with the same person supporting it.

The recommendation, and what it costs Option A. The reason to run a December pilot at all is to stop promising dates the client's calendar will not support, and Option B spends that credibility again in January. The price of A is one retainer month - the first billed month moves from February to March - against a product that goes live on two real cycles of evidence instead of none. This is Ali and Ram's call, not a scheduling detail, because it moves revenue and it moves what William is quoting against.

What is assumed, and what is not known

  • Assumed: the 12 November build start happened as committed on the 11 November call. Nothing in this document verifies it.
  • Assumed: two PMs cover a useful share of December's billing. The split of roughly 70 jobs across six to nine PMs is not known, so the pilot could be anywhere from a handful of requisitions to twenty.
  • Not known: their loaded hourly cost, and their annual billing volume. Both are asks above, and the price should not be defended without them.
  • Not known: whether a second builder is available for January, which is the only thing that makes Option C real.

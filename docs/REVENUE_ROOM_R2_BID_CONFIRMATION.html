Why this is a report and not twelve rows

R2 originally read “write the rows.” The diagnosis changed that. Two services already write to revenue_opportunities; the table is empty for four independent reasons, and fixing any one of them alone still yields zero rows.

  1. The first writer can never run. revenueOpportunityAgent.ts registers itself as a module-load side effect, and nothing imports it — nor any of the other ten files in services/agents/reporting/. There is no dynamic loader. The repo's own cron baseline already records it as confirmed_dead.
  2. The database advertises it as healthy anyway. Its ai_agents row claims an eight-hourly cron, status idle, zero errors. It has zero errors because it has zero runs, and last_run_at is null. Any health surface reading that row sees a working agent.
  3. Its filter uses stage names that do not exist. It matches pipeline_stage IN (qualified, proposal, negotiation); the real vocabulary is new_lead, contacted, enrolled, meeting_scheduled, negotiation, proposal_sent. Two of the three literals are not values of that column.
  4. Even corrected, nothing matches. 1,100 leads score 70 or above and every one sits in an early stage the filter excludes; the three leads in late stages all have a score of zero. Measured match count: 0.

The second writer, the OpenClaw conversion detector, is wired into aiOrchestrator — but nothing calls its exported entry point, it has no ai_agents row at all, and it needs conversations at stage 5 when all 138 rows sit at stage 2 and have not been touched since 2026-04-19.

None of that is fixed by writing bid rows, and a government bid would never satisfy either predicate anyway. The lead scanner is a real and separate defect worth its own ticket: 1,100 high-intent leads are invisible to it.

What happens when you mark the table

  1. Confirmed bids become rows in revenue_opportunities, written by an idempotent sync keyed on delivery_project_id — safe to re-run, no duplicates, going through the existing service rather than a third code path.
  2. Three tiles on the commercialization board light up — qualified pipeline, proposal value out, and the win-rate denominator. That part-pays C8.4 as a side effect.
  3. Anything you mark “not live” stays out, and the test row and the losing half of the duplicate pair get retired so this does not have to be re-litigated next month.
  4. The Room shows value where you gave one and “unknown” where you did not. No tile will display a number nobody measured.

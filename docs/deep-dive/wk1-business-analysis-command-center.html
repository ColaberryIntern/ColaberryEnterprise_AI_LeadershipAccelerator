Overview · Mission

Why Business Analysis exists

A business feels a problem ("we're losing takeout orders to third-party apps that take 30%") but can't hand a team a blueprint. Business Analysis is the bridge: it elicits the real need, analyzes the current process, and specifies what the solution must do — in language both the business and the builders trust. Skip it and you build a beautiful answer to the wrong question.

Business value
  • Prevents the most expensive defect: building the wrong thing.
  • Turns vague asks into testable, prioritized requirements.
  • Creates one shared source of truth everyone can point to.
  • Makes scope — and scope changes — explicit and negotiable.
Common misconceptions
  • "BA is just writing tickets." It's deciding what's worth building.
  • "Requirements = features." Requirements include the why and the non-functionals (speed, security).
  • "The customer knows exactly what they want." They know the pain; BA finds the need.
  • "Do it once." Requirements evolve; traceability keeps them honest.
How AI changes BA. AI can draft user stories, personas, edge cases, and acceptance criteria in seconds. What it can't do is decide the real problem, the priorities, or which stakeholder is right. You supply judgment; AI supplies the first draft.

Section · Good vs Bad

Good vs bad requirements

Good requirements

  • Specific and testable ("results in < 2s for 95% of searches").
  • Trace to a stated business need.
  • Prioritized — not everything is a "Must."
  • Include non-functionals (security, uptime, accessibility).
  • Have acceptance criteria before build starts.

Bad requirements

  • Vague ("make it user-friendly," "should be fast").
  • Untestable — no one can say when it's done.
  • Gold-plated — features nobody asked for.
  • Solution disguised as a need ("add a Kafka queue").
  • No owner, no priority, no trace.
Red flags in AI-drafted requirements: every item marked "Must," acceptance criteria that just restate the story, missing non-functionals, and personas that are demographics instead of goals.
Section · Measurement

KPIs & success metrics

Quality signals

  • % requirements with acceptance criteria
  • Requirements traceable to a need
  • Non-functionals with a measurable target

Health signals

  • Requirement churn rate
  • Defects traced to missing/unclear reqs
  • Stakeholder sign-off rate
Leading vs lagging. Clarity and traceability are leading — they predict trouble early. Defects-traced-to-requirements is lagging — it confirms where discovery fell short.

Architect Review

Before you approve requirements

Run every AI-generated requirement set through this lens.

Problem & scope

  • Is this the real problem, not a solution?
  • Is scope explicit, with a "Won't"?
  • Does each item trace to a need?

Quality

  • Is every story testable?
  • Are non-functionals covered & measurable?
  • Are unhappy paths handled?

Stakeholders

  • Are all key voices represented?
  • Any conflicting needs surfaced?
  • Who signs off?

Priority

  • Is MoSCoW honest?
  • What's the smallest valuable slice?
  • What can wait?
Approve only when: the problem is real, each requirement is testable and traceable, priorities are honest, non-functionals have targets, and a named stakeholder has signed off.
Inspect Your Build

What to check in your Field Guide

After Claude Code builds your Field Guide, open it and check these — this is how you practice the architect's review:

  • BRD — does it open with a business outcome, not a feature list?
  • User stories — does each have acceptance criteria you could actually test?
  • Traceability matrix — can you follow every story back to a need? Any orphans?
  • Personas — are they goals-and-frustrations, or just demographics?
  • Future-state process — does it visibly remove a real pain from the current state?
  • Ask assistant — try asking it "what are the non-functional requirements?" and see if it answers from your docs.

Self-check
1. What's the difference between a functional and a non-functional requirement?
Functional = what the system does; non-functional = how well it does it (speed, security, uptime) — with a measurable target.
2. Rewrite "the app should be fast" as a good requirement.
"Search returns results in under 2 seconds for 95% of queries." Specific, measurable, testable.
3. What does the traceability matrix prevent?
Orphan requirements — anything being built that doesn't trace back to a real business need.
4. Why can't AI own the requirements?
It drafts fast but can't decide the real problem, the priorities, or which stakeholder is right — that's your judgment.

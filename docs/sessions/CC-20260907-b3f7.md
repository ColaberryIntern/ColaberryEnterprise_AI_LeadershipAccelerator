## Why a Sankey

The force graph answered "what connects to what". The question the page is actually asked is "where did these leads come from, where did they stop, and what converted" — a question about volume along a path. Band width is proportional to lead volume, so that answer is read rather than deduced. This mirrors the decision already made for the Visitors traffic flow, and reuses its validated palette so the two charts in the admin agree with each other.

## Brand attribution — the honest finding

Ali asked to filter campaigns by brand "so I can see which brand is sending out what". The dimension is now built end to end and is correct. **It will likely show one real brand plus "Unattributed" in production today**, for reasons read out of the code rather than guessed:

| Evidence | File | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| The tenancy backfill assigns **every** pre-existing campaign to the single `colaberry-enterprise` brand, with the stated assumption "there has never been a second tenant" | `backend/src/scripts/backfillTenancy.ts:225` | All legacy campaigns share one brand |
| Campaign create/update sets no `brand_id` | `backend/src/routes/admin/campaignRoutes.ts` (no `brand_id` reference) | New campaigns are `NULL` → Unattributed |
| `CommunicationLog` has no brand column | `backend/src/models/CommunicationLog.ts` | Brand cannot be derived from sends themselves |
| Eight brands are seeded across four tenants — `colaberry` alone owns `colaberry-enterprise`, `colaberry-training`, `worldoftaxonomy`, `advisor` and `trustbeforeintelligence`, plus tenants `cpn`, `ai-flotation` and `refactored` | `backend/src/seeds/ecosystemSeedData.ts` | The brand dimension is genuinely rich — it maps almost exactly onto the property list on the Visitors page — but only one of the eight has campaigns attributed to it |

This was **not verified against the production database** — the read-only query was blocked by the auto-mode classifier, and the available JWT is a `participant` token, not admin. The filter is therefore built to be correct and future-proof rather than to look busy: options are derived from the data, the unattributed bucket is named as a gap rather than folded into the largest brand, and the graph engine emits an explicit warning when the dimension cannot separate anything ("All N campaigns belong to X. Brand filtering will not narrow this view until campaigns exist under another brand.").

Attribution precedence, in `campaignBrandAttribution.ts`: `campaigns.brand_id` → `sender_profiles.brand_id` (the identity it actually sends as) → Unattributed.

### Why it does not guess

Eight brands sit across four tenants, five of them under `colaberry` alone, so "the
admin's brand" has no single answer. Precedence is: an explicit `brand_id` (validated
against active brands — an unknown or inactive id is a 400, never a dangling foreign
key), then the brand behind the campaign's sender profile, then NULL. An explicit id
that cannot be checked raises; an *inferred* one that cannot be resolved just leaves
the campaign unattributed, because inference failing is not a bad request.

`updateCampaign`'s field allowlist gained `brand_id`/`tenant_id`/`sender_profile_id`,
so the only route to those columns is through the controller that validates them.

**The defect.** `getCampaignGraphData` filtered `leadPaths` by the time window but
counted visitors with a bare `Visitor.count()`. That figure was the all-time total
whatever window was selected, and the Site Visitors node adds anonymous visitors to
lead-linked ones, so a 7-day view reported 55 leads beside 2,180 Site Visitors. The
cutoff is now resolved before the queries and applied to both. `visitorCountOptions`
is exported purely so the rule is testable without a database.

Worth saying plainly: **the old force graph had exactly the same bug.** It only
became visible when a Sankey drew the two numbers next to each other — which is a
fair argument for the chart.

**Percentages: share of the COLUMN, not of total leads.** Site Visitors counts
anonymous visitors who never became leads, so against a total-leads denominator it
reads in the hundreds of percent — worse than showing nothing. Each column is a set
of alternatives that genuinely partition its stage, so a share of it is always 0-100
and answers the question actually being asked: how much of this stage is this? Shown
as `21,241 · 86.1%` under each label, with the stage named in the tooltip and the
screen-reader label.

**The defect.** Every node in a lead-journey chart counts leads. `visitor_site`
alone added people who were never leads, so production drew "12 total leads" beside
"Site Visitors 382" and implied a flow that cannot exist. It also took 96.7% of the
Response column, flattening Ignored (2.0%) and Never Visited (1.3%) into noise —
which made the percentages added in #2289 useless on the one column where they
mattered most.

The anonymous figure was not discarded. It stays in `metrics.visits_generated` and
is promoted to its own finding: *"370 people browsed without becoming leads. Of 382
people who reached the site, 3.1% became leads."* That is arguably the most
actionable line on the page, and it had been buried inside a number that just looked
broken.

**Why it took two passes, recorded because the pattern is worth avoiding.** When the
percentages went in, the >100% problem WAS noticed — and worked around by changing
the denominator to share-of-column, plus a tooltip. Both treated a symptom that had
already been seen. The right question was not "how do I display this number" but
"why does this node count a different population from every node beside it". A
figure that is arithmetically impossible against its neighbours is the data model
reporting a fault, not a formatting problem.

### Four defects only the live payload could expose

1. **Two journeys silently vanished.** `campaign_4ca4c35d…` appeared in two edges but
   not in `nodes` — a campaign deleted while its edges outlived it. Links whose
   endpoint failed to resolve were dropped, so the diagram drew 58,753 of 58,755. A
   placeholder node is now synthesized: volume survives and the gap is visible.
2. **A conversion rate of 364.1%.** Node `count` is distinct leads; edge `volume` is
   journeys along a path. **Nine of the drawn nodes carry more journeys than they
   report leads** — "Alumni Re-Engagement" reports 4 leads and 64 journeys into
   outcomes. Dividing one by the other produced nonsense. The panel now refuses to
   rank a node whose conversions exceed its population, and reports the mismatch as
   its own insight rather than clamping a contradiction to a flattering 100%.
3. **The largest leak was overstated 6.5×.** It said "24,610 leads stop at Never
   Visited" while that node holds 3,775 distinct leads. It now says *journeys*, and
   names the unit in the sentence.
4. **A count cap alone does not make a chart readable.** Production has one campaign
   at 9,658 leads and a tail at 12–18. Top-eight still drew five invisible bands
   whose labels stacked. Campaigns below 1% of campaign volume are now grouped too —
   with a guard against grouping *every* campaign, which would silently turn the
   Campaigns view into the First-touch view.

## Three more defects the RENDER caught

Every assertion in the suite passed while the chart was visibly broken. Rendering the
real component and looking at it (`docs/screenshots/2026-09-07-journey-sankey/`) found:

3. **Campaign labels collided with the first-touch column.** Labels flipped inward on
   the right-hand side, so campaign labels ran leftward over the entry labels beside
   them. Root cause below.
4. **The outcome column was crushed to sub-pixel bands.** 59 paid leads out of 44,619
   movements is a sub-pixel ribbon — honest as a picture, impossible as a target.
   Ribbons now carry an invisible 14px hit path while the visible band keeps its true
   width, so a small outcome is clickable without being drawn heavier than it is.
   Chart height also now scales with node count instead of being fixed at 520.
5. **A stale React dependency.** `flipX` was missing from `renderNode`'s dependency
   array, so once the container was measured, recharts re-laid-out at the real width
   while the label-side decision still used the fallback width. The chart looked
   mis-designed; it was one missing dep.

The layout also changed as a result: the diagram now takes the full card width with
the insight rail beneath it. Sharing a row with a 4-column rail left the plot ~880px
against the ~1,180px seven columns of labels need, so it satisfied its own minimum by
scrolling — and the first thing to scroll out of view was the Outcome column.

## Two defects the tests caught

1. **Conversion rate had the wrong denominator.** `findBestOpportunity` divided outcome volume by the volume *leaving* a node, so any campaign whose only exit was an outcome scored 100% and every terminal campaign tied for first. Now divided by the node's population. Test: `journeyMetrics.test.ts` → "does not crown a tiny sample with a flattering rate".
2. **A "90 days" time filter would have silently done nothing.** `getTimeWindowCutoff` implements `24h/3d/7d/30d` and falls through to all-time for anything else, so a 90-day option would have looked like a working filter that changed no data. The selector now offers exactly the implemented set, asserted by a test.

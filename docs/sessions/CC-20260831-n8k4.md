## Why this was needed

Sessions on 2026-08-17 (W4 Arch) and 2026-08-24 (W5 Arch) both ran past the end and
the final two segments were never reached. The cause was not pace, it was volume.

| | W4 authored | W4 as presented (override) | W5 authored | **W6 authored** | **W6 tonight** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| teach slides | 22 | 22 | 22 | 21 | **16** |
| body prose (chars) | 13,787 | 11,450 | 15,435 | 14,903 | **8,762** |
| live micro-builds | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | **2** |
| rendered slides | — | — | — | 47 | **38** |

Even the hand-tuned Week 4 override, which Ali shaped himself, was still 11,450
characters of prose and four live builds. The build window is 30 minutes and four
substantial builds do not fit in it. That is the overrun, and it is structural.

## The four decisions

**1. One narrative spine: the night shift.**
Week 5 built a helper that works while you watch. Tonight it works while you sleep.
The four protocol upgrades stop being a list and become the four things any night-shift
worker needs:

| Night shift | Protocol |
|---|---|
| 🔑 No keys of their own | sampling |
| 📞 They call in | progress + log notifications |
| 🚪 They stay on their floor | roots |
| 📋 Somebody signed the posting | transport + state |

Restated in the roadmap slide, in every architecture eyebrow (`Upgrade 1 of 4` …
`4 of 4`), and at the close. The existing "2 AM — loud or quiet?" device is kept as
the exam question and written on the whiteboard for the whole night.

**2. Prose cut 41%, slides 21 → 16.** Merged progress + log notifications into one
slide, merged demo-shaped/loud-or-quiet, folded the trust ladder into the roadmap as a
spoken line rather than its own slide, and dropped the sampling-trace autopsy (the most
abstract of the three, and the only one nothing later depends on).

**3. Four live builds → two, and the other two became teaching prompts.**
Built live: progress notifications (fast, visible, the highest-satisfaction moment of
the night) and the graded transport decision record. Logging and roots became
`kind: 'review'` prompts — the full prompt is on screen and taught line by line, with
the *reasoning written into the prompt itself as comments* (`# WHY: without the
declaration the client drops every log message on the floor…`), and the room runs them
before Thursday. This is the single biggest time saving, and it is the mechanism Ali
asked for: teaching survives on steps where building does not.

**4. The theater poll moved from minute 95 to minute 45.** The transport decision is
the best moment of the night. Authored, it sat in the `challenge` segment at minute 95
— exactly where the overrun lands, so it kept getting cut. It now fires inside
`architecture`, directly after the transport slide that briefs it. The `challenge` and
`trivia` tail is now deliberately expendable: a self-diagnosis poll and a short
knowledge check, either of which can be dropped without losing anything load-bearing.

Questions were **not** reduced (8, same as authored). They cost 1-2 minutes each and
they are what holds the room. The time came out of prose and live builds.

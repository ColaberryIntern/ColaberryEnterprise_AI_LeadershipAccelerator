### Why these paths, and why they are not guessable

`frontend/public/robots.txt` is `User-agent: * / Allow: /`, and the demo contains Middlesex's
company name, their Shawsheen Street address, and five named staff (Jamie Morse, Nick Arena,
Terry). A clean path such as `/aia-billing-demo/` would therefore expose a named client, their
address and their people to the open web.

Both pages consequently sit behind an unguessable path segment and carry
`<meta name="robots" content="noindex,nofollow">`. The meta tag is used rather than an nginx
`add_header`, because `try_files` drops `add_header` in this config. R3's actual requirement is
only that the link "opens on a click" with "no sign-in needed", and a long path satisfies that;
the trust signal Jamie needs is the `enterprise.colaberry.ai` domain, not a short path.

The demo and the briefing are on **separate** tokens on purpose. The demo is client-facing and
will be sent to Jamie, Terry, Chris, Brian and Nick. The briefing carries our pricing, margin
reasoning and the ROI model, and must never be reachable from anything shared with the client.

### No nginx change was required

`nginx/nginx.conf` ends with `location / { try_files $uri $uri/ /index.html; }`, so a real file
resolves before the SPA fallback. Proven against production rather than assumed:
`https://enterprise.colaberry.ai/linkedin-video-thumbnail.html` already returns **200**, which
is the same mechanism this change relies on.

### The relative demo link had to be rewritten

The briefing linked the demo twice as a bare relative `MIDDLESEX_DEMO_AS_SENT.html`, which only
resolved when both files sat in one folder - the fragile arrangement the e-mail had to ask William
to reproduce by hand. Both links now point at the hosted demo URL.

### What was wrong

with **Jamie approving in the middle of the chain**. Her words on the call: *"it was me, then it
was the PM writes all what they want... It's three approvers. I'm not included as one of the
approvers. I'm the end person."* And on the button that read *Send to accounting*: *"but it really
would end up going to you. Okay, so there's one additional person."*

### Five seats, not four

R1 anticipated "a fourth seat". The chain actually needs **five**, and this is a deliberate
deviation from the ticket rather than an oversight. R2 records that Brian Dempster and Nick Arena
are **two of the three** approvers with a third unidentified, so the sequence is project manager,
three approvers, Jamie. Reading "fourth seat" literally would have required folding the project
manager in as approver one, which contradicts R2.

### The third approver is shown, not guessed

R1 says to build the chain now and leave the third seat unnamed rather than wait on R2. It renders

### State-model changes

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| `ready` = "Ready to send" | `ready` = "Delivered to accounting" |
| `sent` = "Submitted" | `sent` = "Submitted to the GC" |

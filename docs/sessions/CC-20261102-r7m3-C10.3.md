## Why stock and flow had to be separated

They are both called "the enrollment target" and they are not convertible by division.
Stock is a level; flow is a rate that depends on churn and on the horizon allowed to reach
the level, neither of which appears in a stock number. At the measured churn, replacement
rivals growth, which is exactly what a single blended target hides.

## The churn problem, stated rather than smoothed over

Production gives two readings in different units and flow consumes a monthly rate:

| reading | value | implies monthly churn |
|---|---|---|
| median tenure before cancellation | 34 days | **0.4624** |
| observed survival, 25 of 165 monthly subscriptions | 15.2% | **0.8485** (over an unknown window, so not strictly a monthly rate) |

Flow scales linearly in churn, so the answer differs materially:

| | nominal | realization-adjusted (46.3%) |
|---|---|---|
| stock (independent of churn) | **212** | **458** |
| flow, median-tenure churn | **29/month** | **50/month** |
| flow, observed-survival churn | **40/month** | **61/month** |

Both are tested. The spread is the honest output: anyone quoting one number for "the
monthly enrollment target" is choosing a churn model without saying so.

Inputs: loaded monthly cost $40,612 (36,200 loaded payroll + 4,412 opex), plan mix 25
monthly and 4 annual, 29 current paying members, 12-month horizon.

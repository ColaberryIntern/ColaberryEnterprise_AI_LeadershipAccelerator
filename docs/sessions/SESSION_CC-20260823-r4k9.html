2026-08-23
What changednew `TRUST_BEFORE_INTELLIGENCE_INTEGRATION.md`; corrected the D-04 caveat in `DOMAIN_REUSE_MAP.md`, the layer names and warning box in `TARGET_ARCHITECTURE.md` diagram 7, and the Gate 5 row in `TEST_PLAN.md`.
Verificationread `manuscript/` at `main` of `colaberry/trust-before-intelligence-book` (public) — chapters 2 (INPACT), 4/5/6 (the seven layers) and 7 (GOALS). Confirmed the master plan's vocabulary is correct, and that the book's canonical layer names are fuller than the plan's shorthand: **Layer 1 Multi-Modal Storage**, **Layer 2 Real-Time Data**. `archive/` holds superseded drafts and is explicitly not the source.
Notes**the plan's caution inverted the real finding.** It says "do not invent scores if the book does not define them" — the book **does** define them, exactly: INPACT is **1–6 per dimension** (36 max, reported on a 100-point scale), GOALS is **1–5 per dimension** (25 max) on a named maturity ladder (Absent/Basic/Developing/Proficient/Advanced). So the risk was never inventing a score, it was inventing a *different* one and quietly diverging from the framework Colaberry sells. Two further constraints surfaced: the INPACT dependency order (Instant → Natural+Permitted → Contextual → Adaptive+Transparent) is a **build constraint** — a story graph scheduling Adaptive before Instant is invalid, not merely ambitious; and GOALS must be stored as five separate scores, never averaged, because averaging hides the single-dimension collapse the framework exists to catch. The book also ties minimum GOALS thresholds to regulation (EU AI Act 2024/1689 Arts. 9–15, NIST AI RMF), which is the shape `DeliveryProfile` should take at Gate 13 — thresholds, not a compliance claim. **Still unread:** Chapter 9 (`measuring_agent_readiness`, fetch timed out) and the appendix scoring methodology — both needed before the assessment UI is built.

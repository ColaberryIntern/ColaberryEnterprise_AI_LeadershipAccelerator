## What "attach the knowledge base" turned out to mean

All three KB tables were empty in production: `cora_courses`, `cora_cohorts`,
`cora_kb_entries`, plus `responsible_persons`. The tables existed, so the migration had
run; the seed never had. `coraAgentService` asks `kbService` for course slug `ai-architect`,
found nothing, and fell back to a prompt that instructs Cora to acknowledge receipt and route
the sender to support@colaberry.com — the inbox they had just written to.

`dist/seeds/seedKbData.js` was already in the image. It is idempotent (`findOrCreate` only,
no `destroy`/`TRUNCATE`/`DELETE` anywhere — checked before running). Running it created
1 course, 1 cohort, 8 responsible persons and 26 entries, with prices imported from
`MEMBERSHIP_PRICE` rather than typed, so $149 annual / $199 month-to-month are right by
construction.

**The seed alone would have made things worse, in four ways.** It ships the Founding Cohort
of July 2026, and `buildCoraSystemPromptFromDB` injects the cohort line into *every* prompt:

1. **Stale dates everywhere.** Open house 16 Jul, start 23 Jul, end 15 Oct, Expo October,
   32 of 40 seats. It is 15 November. Corrected from the live `cohorts` row — Cohort -
   November 2026, open, started 2026-11-12, 125 seats with 2 taken. Dates production does not
   record (cohort end, Expo, next open house) were set **NULL**, and the four answers that
   interpolate them deactivated, because merge tags resolve NULL to a visible `[TBD]` and a
   placeholder in a customer email is its own defect.
2. **A wrong schedule.** The seed claimed "Monday (Architecture Day, 2 hours) and Thursday
   (Build Day, 2 hours) — 4 hours total per week". The live cohort says core Monday
   6:30–8:00 PM America/Chicago with Thursday as an **optional** lab. Billing an optional lab
   as a mandatory second class misleads someone deciding whether they can commit. Rewritten
   from the cohort row.
3. **A compliance violation.** The Open House answer said the event "is completely free".
   TWC §807.172(d) does not allow a career school to advertise training at no cost. Rewritten
   to "requires no commitment and no card" before it could ever ship. A later pass also
   removed the two remaining "free" mentions (describing Docker and GitHub), which are
   outside the rule's purpose but not worth leaving in her mouth.
4. **Invented pricing claims.** "this founding rate is permanently locked in for you" and
   "enroll early to secure your spot and lock the founding rate". The founding cohort ended
   in October and $149 is the standing annual price, so both were false scarcity plus a
   promise about future pricing nobody authorised. Removed.

## The one that mattered most: she invented a refund policy

Asked directly, Cora answered **"we do not offer refunds for the membership fees once they
have been paid"**, and for annual members "the plan runs for the full annual term".

Nothing in the knowledge base says that. She extrapolated it from the cancellation entry's
"your plan runs for the full annual term". The refund policy has been an open gap since June
(`directives/cora-knowledge-base-gaps.md` item 2) carrying the explicit note *do not ship a
guessed policy* — and the DB-backed prompt has no scope or escalation guard beyond one
closing sentence, so nothing stopped her filling the silence.

Fixed with a new entry whose answer states **no terms at all** and routes to a person, so the
model has something to say. It comes back `needs_human = true`.

**Follow-up worth a ticket, not done here:** the static `buildCoraSystemPrompt` had scope
guards and escalation triggers (PR #25, June). `buildCoraSystemPromptFromDB` does not. The
KB-Ops migration dropped them, and a per-question entry is a patch over a missing guard.

## A correction to what I told Ali earlier in this session

I reported 1,290 replies between 18 June and 14 July as the period "when it was working".
The number is real, the reading was wrong: **1,251 were sent on 14 July alone**, the mail-loop
day. The actual working period is **39 replies across three days**.

Of those 39:

- **30 went to our own addresses** — support@, ali@, cora@, Basecamp notifications. Only
  **9 ever reached an outside person**.
- **19 of 39 were outright refusals** ("I cannot assist with inquiries related to…", "this
  does not pertain to the Executive AI Build Accelerator").
- **34 of 39 still needed a human.**

Those refusals were not a fault. They were Ali's own scope decision of 17 June, recorded in
the gaps directive: Accelerator questions only, safe hand-off for everything else. Which
means **June is not the quality bar to aim at** — today's 31 of 42 is already past it.

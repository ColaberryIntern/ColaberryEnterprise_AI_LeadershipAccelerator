## Findings

### 1. Two of the three things the ticket asked for needed infrastructure that does not exist.

The ticket asked, on the transition to satisfied, to set the latch, **check the Basecamp to-do
off**, and **fire a celebration**. The latch exists. The other two did not survive measurement:

- **There is no link from a platform project to its Basecamp board.** `Project` carries no bucket
  id, and the story publisher writes its list id to `<slug>-published.json` **beside the script**,
  a local file the platform never reads. The Basecamp mirror does hold **504 story to-dos**, all
  titled `STORY-NNN`, so a match is feasible, but only after something creates that link, and
  then checking a to-do off is a new outbound write to boards students are working from.
- **`community_notifications` cannot carry a celebration.** Its `source_type` union is `post`,
  `comment`, `friendship`, `dm`, and it holds **no message text at all**. The ticket wants a
  sentence that names the artifact, which that table structurally cannot store without widening a
  social feed to carry build events.

Raised with Ali rather than resolved quietly. He chose the gate plus the celebration, with the
Basecamp check-off split out as its own ticket. A3 therefore has its own table.

### 2. Nothing in production carries a contract, so the gate is inert today, and that is the point.

Measured: **0 of 67 published plans mention artifacts**, while **570 stories have been verified
across 41 projects**. So the completion path is well exercised and the gate will change nothing
until contracts exist, which is A5's retrofit. The mechanism ships before the data, as A2 did.

This is also why the no-contract rule had to be exactly right. If a story without a contract were
gated rather than ignored, switching the flag on would freeze every live board at once.

### 3. The two central rules point in opposite directions, on purpose.

**Nothing earned is ever revoked.** An already-latched story stays complete whatever the
repository holds now, and that is checked FIRST so no later rule can reach past it.

**Nothing is granted on absence of evidence.** An unreadable tree leaves the story open.

That second one is the OPPOSITE of `criterionPaths`, which deliberately enforces nothing against a
student when the tree cannot be read, so a timeout never costs them a criterion. Both are the
cautious direction. The costs are not symmetric: there, being wrong takes something away; here,
being wrong hands out a completion nobody verified, and the whole workstream exists because "done"
had stopped meaning anything. Rule 1 is what keeps this from ever taking anything away.

### 4. The celebration had to be computed in one place and written in another.

Gating required moving the artifact check to BEFORE the story loop, because the loop needs the
decision. But a story completes only when its acceptance criteria pass AND its artifacts are in
place, and the acceptance half is judged inside that loop. Writing the celebration where it is
computed would have congratulated someone whose scenarios then failed.

So it is computed before the loop, where the contract and the artifact names live, and written
after the latch through `fireStoryCelebration`. There is a test that the orchestrator does NOT
write it, and a mutation proving that test fails if it starts to.

### 5. ts-jest hid a real type error behind nine passing tests, again.

`storyCelebration.test.ts` looped over three object literals as a `Record<string, string>`, and the
inferred union of them is not assignable. Nine tests passed, because ts-jest runs with
`isolatedModules` and transpiles rather than typechecks. The test typecheck gate caught it. Second
time this phase pair; the lesson is that green jest is not evidence about types, and the gate has
to be re-run after every change rather than once per phase.

### 6. Three existing suites mocked `StudentTask` without `findAll`, and the tempting fix was wrong.

A3 reads the already-latched story ids once before the loop. Three verification suites mock the
model with only `findOne` and `update`, so they died with `findAll is not a function`.

Fixed by adding `findAll` to the mocks. The alternative was wrapping the read in a try/catch that
defaults to an empty set, which would have been quieter and worse: a database hiccup would then
silently weaken the rule whose own comment says it is checked first, which is the exact pattern of
quiet degradation this workstream keeps removing.

## What A3 does not claim

- **Nothing was deployed, and no row was written to any production database.** The only database
  touched was `accelerator_dev1`, inside a transaction that was rolled back.
- **It is off by default, in two steps.** `celebrate` computes the decision and fires celebrations
  and withholds nothing; `enforce` additionally stops a story completing. The second exists so
  the first can measure what it would have held back, and A3's flag is separate from A2's, with a
  test that neither can switch on the other.
- **No story has ever been held, and no celebration has ever fired**, because nothing in
  production carries a contract and the flag is off.
- **The Basecamp to-do is not checked off.** See finding 1; it is its own ticket.
- **The celebration has no surface yet.** The rows exist and `listStoryCelebrations` reads them;
  nothing in the portal renders them. That is the next thing somebody should build, and the
  artifact registry page (A4, L11.6) is where it belongs.
- **`must_contain` is still not read**, so an artifact recorded as present with unchecked contents
  is celebrated as being in place and explicitly not as being right.

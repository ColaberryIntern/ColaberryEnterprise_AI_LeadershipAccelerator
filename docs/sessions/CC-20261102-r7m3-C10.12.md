## The cliff does not exist in the form the ticket anticipates

Measured read-only against production on 2026-11-02.

C10.12 was raised to get ahead of "the first cohort of yearly members" reaching renewal.
Of **45 annual subscriptions ever written**, only 4 are active:

| status | rows | can it renew? |
|---|---|---|
| pending | 23 | no - never began, no period end at all |
| failed | 17 | no - never began |
| canceled | 1 | no |
| **active** | **4** | **yes** |

So the entire annual cliff is **4 memberships and $7,152 of nominal annual value**:

| renewal month | memberships | nominal | realized at 46.3% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2027-07 | 3 | $5,364 | $2,483.53 |
| 2027-11 | 1 | $1,788 | $827.84 |
| **total** | **4** | **$7,152** | **$3,311.37** |

At the measured realization rate the real exposure is **$3,311**, not $7,152 - a 2.2x
difference in what is actually at stake, and the reason both numbers are reported.

**$3,311 arriving across two months eight months from now does not need a plan.** Saying
so is more useful than building a retention programme around it.

## Where the renewal risk actually is

Three exposures that are real, in order of size:

**1. The legacy subscription book - $13,877 a month, realized.** 124 schedules. This is
**1.9x the entire annual plan's value every single month**. It decays slowly rather than
expiring: still $10,933/mo in December 2027, $8,271/mo by June 2028, and 30 schedules carry
no end date at all. It is exposed not to a cliff but to **mass cancellation if service
visibly stops** when the programme it is attached to is sunset. That makes C6.11 (the
retention offer to the 124 legacy subscribers, before any sunset announcement) the ticket
that actually matters here, and it is due 2026-11-07.

**2. The monthly book renews before the annual one ever does.** All 25 active monthly
memberships, $4,975 nominal, come up between August and December 2026 - every one of them
ahead of the first annual renewal in July 2027. And the median monthly tenure before
cancellation is **34 days**, so this is not a cliff either; it is a continuous leak.

**3. Twenty-six comp accounts carry a period end in 2036.** A ten-year free grant that
nothing in the C-ladder accounts for. Worth $0 in revenue, which is exactly why it is easy
to forget, and worth asking whether a decade was intended.

## Decisions to take before July 2027, such as they are

1. **Whether to keep the annual plan at all.** 45 attempts produced 4 active members and
   40 that never began. That is a **checkout problem, not a pricing problem** - see the
   escalation on unbilled active subscriptions, and C10.1.
2. **C6.11 first, and before any sunset is announced.** The legacy book is the asset. An
   announcement that reaches those 124 subscribers before an offer does is the single
   largest revenue risk in the ladder.
3. **Confirm the four annual renewals are actually billable.** Of the 29 active paid
   subscriptions, 17 carry no PaySimple schedule. Whether these four are among them has
   not been established, and a renewal date on a row that cannot bill is not revenue.

## Why the module takes rows as an argument

It does not query `subscriptions` itself, deliberately. The annual plan and the legacy book
live in different places, and a function that only knew how to find one would keep
reporting a $7,152 cliff while the $13,877-a-month exposure went unmeasured. It also takes
no view of `now`: a function filtering by "upcoming" could not be tested against fixed
expectations and would quietly change its answer every day.

It flags amounts that disagree with the price of record without calling them errors. A
legacy book legitimately carries legacy amounts such as the $250/month training
subscriptions, but an unexplained amount must stay visible, because that is how a wrong
price survives inside a forecast.

### One card is not always one initiative

This is the decision that shaped the module. Six cards are a single initiative — five
hypotheses and membership — and clicking one asks "what has happened towards this?", which a
timeline answers.

The seventh, `gov_bid_pursuit`, is an **aggregate**. It reports a count of bids carrying the
pursuit flag: twelve on 2026-11-03, including a row named "Snow College Adult Learner - T8
test", one RFP duplicated under two project ids, and three rows with no delivery project.
Clicking it cannot produce one timeline, because there is no single thing it is the history
of.

So a drill-down has two shapes. `single` is a timeline. `roster` is a list of the
initiatives the card aggregates, each with its own evidence count and the flags its own
record shows, each clickable through to its own `single` view at
`?bid=<delivery_project_id>`. Collapsing them would have meant inventing a merged timeline
across twelve unrelated bids, or quietly picking one — and either would have looked fine on
screen.

**The duplicate flag is computed across the roster, not per row.** A per-row check sees each
copy of a duplicated RFP as unique and flags neither. Counting titles first flags both.

### The timeline reads only the spine

It would have been quicker to query Basecamp, email and the proposal tables directly here
and merge the results. That is the design R3 exists to replace: several bespoke queries that
each decide for themselves what counts as an event, with no way to unlink a wrong one and no
record of who linked it. Everything reads `evidence_links`, and anything that wants to
appear earns its place by being linked.

### Why the backfill is in this ticket rather than R5

Between R3 and R4 sat a problem: nothing wrote a link, so every timeline rendered empty and
the page could only be verified against fixtures. A surface checked only against fixtures is
one nobody has seen work.

`gov_proposal_documents` is the one evidence source needing no ingestion — already in this
database, already attached to a `delivery_project_id` by foreign key, and the only signal
that distinguishes a real pursuit from a flagged row. So it is both the cheapest first
producer and the most useful. R5 remains the scheduled Basecamp ingestion and R8 email; this
is a backfill of something already present, not a second sync.

It is safe on every boot because it goes through `linkEvidence`, so R3's guarantees apply
unchanged: the unique index makes a repeat run a no-op, and a document Ali unlinked stays
unlinked. **Refusals are counted and reported, not swallowed** — "nothing to do" and "I was
correctly refused fourteen times" are different facts and only one is boring.

`observed_at` is `COALESCE(drafted_at, created_at)`. The document list is *materialized*, so
`created_at` is when somebody pressed a button, not when the document was written.
`drafted_at` is the event a timeline is asking about. Same discipline as R3's schema, for the
same reason: the R2 diagnosis found a conversion rate counting backwards from exactly this
conflation.

Confidence is **1**, because this is a foreign key rather than an inference — the document
*is* attached to that project by the database. Anything lower would imply a judgment was
made. And `source_url` is left null rather than constructed from a guessed admin route,
because a dead link in front of a reader is worse than no link.

### An empty timeline explains itself

The spine is new and only one producer writes to it, so most timelines are genuinely empty.
That is exactly the point where a page starts lying — by rendering nothing and letting the
reader supply the reason. So three cases are kept apart in words: the table is missing, the
table is present and holds nothing for this initiative, or here is what happened.

## 2026-11-04 (later) — Ali corrected the government card: scoped, not annotated

**Ali:** *"I don't agree with this 'The government card is an aggregate of twelve flagged
bids'. I only want the projects that are in motion and have been assigned right now."*

He is right, and not only as a preference. The first design listed all twelve flagged rows
and attached warnings — *this one looks like a test row, that title appears twice, this one
has no documents*. That makes the reader do the filtering, and a reader who skims does it
wrong. Annotating a wrong number does not make it the right one.

### Measuring the instruction produced a sharper definition than the annotation ever was

A bid is **in motion and assigned right now** when it is at
`decision = approved_bid_pursuit` AND `status = active` AND it holds at least one
`delivery_project_members` row with `revoked_at IS NULL`.

`delivery_project_members` holds **8 rows in the entire system: 6 revoked, 2 live.** The two
live ones sit on:

| Bid | Project | Assigned | Documents |
|---|---|---|---|
| RFP — Enterprise Work Order Management System | `80b35be2` | 2026-10-28, `associate_builder` | 1, drafted |
| 2026 Saratoga Springs Municipal Asset Management RFP | `3867b241` | 2026-10-28, `associate_builder` | 3, none drafted |

**Exactly the two Ali named as his active proposals.** The definition and his own statement
agree without being made to.

It also settles three things the annotated version could only flag:

- The **"Snow College Adult Learner - T8 test"** row has no live assignment. Excluded by
  the definition rather than warned about.
- Of the **RFP duplicated** under two project ids, `80b35be2` holds the live assignment and
  the drafted document. The other copy is excluded.
- **Statewide Victim-Centered** has the most documents of anything in the system (5, two
  drafted) and its membership is **revoked**. So it is not in motion. This resolves the
  puzzle from the R2 diagnosis, where I reported that "the one with the most documents is
  the one he did not name" as if it were an inconsistency on Ali's side. It was not.
  **Document count was the misleading signal; assignment is not.**

### `delivery_projects.status` is deliberately NOT the test for "in motion"

Every one of the twelve flagged bids reads `discovery`. The four projects reading `building`
are two sandbox demos and two government projects that are not pursuit bids at all and carry
no members. A strict "building AND assigned" intersection is **empty**, so using that column
would have produced a card showing zero while two bids were genuinely being worked.

### The assertion changed target rather than being deleted

`assertGovPipelineIsNotPresentedAsConfirmed` forbade this card from ever being `live`, on
the grounds that *"no column records which bids a human confirmed."* Measuring Ali's
correction showed that premise was wrong: granting a `delivery_project_members` row **is** a
recorded human act — it carries `granted_by_identity_id` and `granted_at` — and revoking it
is another.

So the card is now `live`, and the guard became
`assertGovPipelineCountsOnlyAssignedBids`, which fails if the provenance ever stops
requiring a live assignment. What must never happen is this card drifting back to counting
the raw flag, because that set has contained a test row.

### Other changes that followed

- The card's label is now **"Government bids in motion"**, not "in pursuit".
- The roster flags describe **work rather than suspicion**: who holds it, since when, how
  much is drafted, whether it has gone out. The owner's name leads each row, because it is
  why the bid is on the list.
- A missing `delivery_project_members` table degrades to **"not yet measurable"** rather
  than falling back to the flag count — falling back would silently reinstate exactly the
  behavior being removed. Tested in both directions.
- Zero in-motion bids now reads differently from zero flagged bids: *"12 carry the pursuit
  flag but none has a live assignment"* is a real and useful state.
- The roster query uses an **inner** `JOIN delivery_projects` rather than a left join plus
  an `IS NOT NULL` filter, so the three flagged rows with no project are excluded
  structurally instead of by a clause someone could drop.

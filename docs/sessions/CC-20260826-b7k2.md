## The contradiction — she was right, our earlier explanation was wrong

We told her that 5:12 of a 7:58 video showing "Watched 33%" was the anti-skip rule
working as designed, and that only continuous playback counts. She tested it and
reported she had to rewatch the whole video to reach 75%.

Her actual production row (`timeline_card_progress.analytics.watch`, enrollment
`3280b47e-67b7-411a-8926-4f9c3b86c97d`, card `7190d6ec` "Large Language Models
explained briefly"):

```json
{"provider":"youtube","watched_s":157.4,"duration_s":478,"watched_pct":33,
 "max_position_s":316,"last_beat_at":"2026-08-25T14:42:15.332Z"}
```

- The denominator was **correct**: 478s = 7:58, pinned from ground truth. This was
  never the estimated_time bug.
- The **numerator** is the defect: her playhead reached 316s and only **157.4s were
  credited** — 49.8% of the time she actually played. Had every played second
  counted she would have read 66%, not 33%.
- Progress does **persist** (server accumulates `p.watched_s + delta`; her row spans
  2026-07-23 to 2026-08-25). Nothing was wiped. But the bar renders 0% on every
  load because no endpoint reads the stored value back, so it looks wiped.

Cause: `frontend/src/components/timeline/VideoEmbed.tsx:52,86` —
`MAX_TICK_DELTA_S = 2.5`, and `if (d > 0 && d < MAX_TICK_DELTA_S) buf.delta += d`.
Any gap over 2.5s between position samples is discarded as "a seek". It cannot
distinguish a scrub-drag from buffering, an ad, a throttled tab, or a second
autoplaying player on the same page. All of those are real watching and all earn
nothing.

**Reproduced live in a real browser** against production (script:
`scratchpad/verify_swati.js`, all controls passing): 90s of continuous playback in
a clean automated browser credited only **78.4s — 13% lost under ideal conditions**
with no ads, no buffering and no tab switching.

Platform-wide: of 369 sessions where the playhead reached >=95% of the real video
length, **23 are still below the 75% bar**.

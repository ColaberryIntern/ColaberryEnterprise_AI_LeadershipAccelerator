## Three things the measurements changed

**1. The compliance problem the ticket was built around is already gone.** Both tickets name
"Welcome to Your Free AI Preview" as the reason a quote-time gate is needed: the most-selected
asset, 392 of 416 content selections, active, email-allowed, advertising our own offering as
free. Measured on 2026-11-04, asset `4f848072-9fcd-47ac-a1c2-c633eab7e0f8` is now titled
**"Welcome to Your AI Preview"**. The word is gone. The gate still ships, because the library is
1,444 rows Marketing edits without passing through CI, so "clean today" is a reading rather than
a property. Both halves of that story are pinned in a test so neither is lost.

**2. `approval_status` is an unenforced column.** The decision says Maya may quote "approved"
assets. Measured: the column is NULL on **all 1,444 rows**, no row has ever been approved, and
nothing in the repo writes it. `approval_status = 'approved'` would have selected **zero** assets
and shipped a quoting feature that can quote nothing while looking rigorous. Ali chose active +
email-allowed + the gate on 2026-11-04, knowing that. The absence is recorded as
`APPROVAL_IS_UNENFORCED` and pinned by a test rather than left in a comment.

**3. One clause in the new query is a no-op, and says so.** `'email' = ANY(allowed_channels)`
excludes exactly zero rows: the column defaults to `{email}` and nothing has ever set it
otherwise. It is kept, because the day a row is marked sms-only this query must already respect
it, but the docblock records it as a no-op rather than describing it as protection. The control
that was meant to prove the clause excludes something proved the opposite, which is why it is
written down instead of implied.

## What the SQL actually selects, measured not assumed

Of 1,444 rows: 472 active, and every later clause costs nothing — 472 after email, 472 after both
date clauses, 472 after the title check. The gate then refuses **5** of those 472. All five are
editorial or third-party references (*Google Colab — Free Jupyter Notebooks*, *The Easiest FREE
AI Tools for Beginners*, *Training-Free Contrastive Decoding*), not Colaberry offers. The matcher
cannot tell an offer from a reference, so it refuses them; that cost is pinned in a test as known
rather than left to be discovered as a bug.

Worth contrast: `bannedCopyInstruction`'s docblock records a live Explorer render refusing 8 of
12 drafts. That was model-written prose. Over asset titles the refusal rate is about 1%, so the
gate does not gut the library.

## What this deliberately does NOT do

- **It does not compose, draft, queue or send.** It returns a screened list and a digest.
- **It does not register a Maya tool.** Wiring it into the tool registry changes what an agent
  can do and deserves its own review.
- **It does not include campaign templates (library B).** The decision named them, but the
  codebase has two candidate homes: `follow_up_sequences.steps[].body_template`, which the sender
  really uses, and `content_templates`, which has no service, controller or route consumer
  anywhere in `backend/src`. Choosing by guess would author a decision nobody made.

## Absence reported as absence

`quotable: []` is ambiguous on its own, so the result type keeps `refused` and `considered`
beside it: a caller can tell "the library is empty" from "the library had rows and every one was
refused". The digest says two different sentences for those two states, because a model told
"no material" when twenty rows were refused will invent copy to fill a gap it was told was
natural. A mutation collapsing the two sentences is killed by a test.

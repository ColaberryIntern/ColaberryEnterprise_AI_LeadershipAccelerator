## Investigation: why mail is in Trash rather than Archive (no code change)

Ruled out, with evidence, in this order:

1. **Gmail filters** - `users.settings.filters.list` returns exactly 2 filters: a
   LinkedIn-to-`c_interviews` labeler, and one on `to:ali+999@colaberry.com` removing
   UNREAD+INBOX (the bot-login alias). Neither adds TRASH.
2. **Our own code** - re-grepped every label-write site on `origin/main`, not just literal
   `'TRASH'`. Seven sites; three add a label resolved from a variable, which the first pass
   missed. Both external inputs are clean: `WATCHER_FILE_LABEL` is unset in the prod container
   (defaults to `Inbox Intel/Watcher Handled`), and `inbox_case_actions` holds **0** rows whose
   payload mentions trash. `autoArchiveService` only ever removes INBOX. Nothing calls
   `messages.trash`.
3. **Read state** - the decisive test. Of 80 sampled messages in `in:trash newer_than:2d`,
   **74 were READ before deletion** and only 6 unread. Controls both ways: `in:inbox` shows a
   34/66 unread mix, so UNREAD detection works; `in:spam` is 28/28 unread, so mail filed
   automatically at delivery looks nothing like this.

**Conclusion: this is deliberate human triage, not a bug.** Mail is being read and then
Deleted where Archive was meant. Volume is 14-115 messages/day. Top senders over 14 days:
`ali@colaberry.com` 294, `discussions@school.colaberry.com` 148, `notifications@app.basecamp.com`
59. The repeat-offender conversations are our own daily automated reports (intern filings,
Intern Nudges, CB Quality, Billing, the LandJet/ShipCES dailies).

Two things that fall out of it, neither fixed here:

- The 294 `ali@colaberry.com` items include the BCC receipts that
  `feedback_always_bcc_ali_and_leave_the_copy_in_his_inbox` says must stay. They are being
  deleted within minutes, so that rule is not holding in practice.
- `inboxSyncService` syncs with `q: 'newer_than:1d -in:sent'` and **no `in:inbox` filter**, so
  `inbox_emails` mirrors trash and spam alongside inbox mail. This invalidated an earlier
  inbox-passage query of mine (100% of *spam* appeared to have "passed through the inbox",
  which is not credible) and may mean the Inbox COS pipeline is classifying spam as inbox mail.
  Worth its own ticket.

### Intern Delivery Command Center: scoped to intern project work, per-person attribution, and a copyable Basecamp message from Ali (2026-08-25)

  - Date: 2026-08-25

  - What changed: Four asks from Ali, two new modules and five edited files, no schema and no deploy. **(1) Scope.** New `backend/src/scripts/lib/internDeliveryScope.js` decides which Basecamp todolists count as intern project work and is now the single source of truth for who is an intern; the person-exclusion logic previously duplicated in `internDeliveryData.js` moved here so the harvest and the list classifier cannot disagree. The rule is deliberately mostly derived rather than an allow-list of ids, so a new intern project appears the day it is created: a list is dropped if it is one of **five explicitly denied ids**, if its name matches an onboarding/orientation pattern, if **no intern holds a task on it**, or if it has fewer than 3 delivery tasks and no release structure. Classification happens in `harvestDelivery` **before** the comment fetch, which is the expensive part (one call per commented todo), so the 31-todo onboarding checklist no longer costs the most and contributes the least. Result on the live snapshot: **25 lists to 14, 41 people to 11**. Held back: `Colaberry Internship Build System` (31 todos, one per person, an onboarding roster), `New Internship Onboarding` (16), `Small Win AI Team` (legacy cohort), `Gov Contracting Eligibility` and `Data Flotation Set-Aside` (staff work sitting in the Gov Contracts bucket: Ram, Srinivas, Vinay, Dheeraj), `Intelligence Generators (9) - BUILD` (30 todos, all Ali's, no intern), and five one-todo stubs (`Cline Projects`, `IPBC Automation`, `Author AI`, `Cora`, `AZURE VM`). What survives is the four Gov Contracts interns Ali named, each with a BUILD and a PROPOSAL pair (Obi/Selective Service, Omolola/Detroit Voter, Akiwam/VA ERP, Samrawit/Fairfax), the five Internship story-driven builds, and `Autonomous`. **Nothing is dropped silently:** every exclusion is carried through the snapshot as `withheld[]` with its reason and rendered as a table in the Method section, so a missing list reads as a decision rather than a broken harvest. **(2) Ownership.** `resolveOwner` became `resolveOwnership` and now refuses to name an owner it cannot justify. One intern holding every delivery task is `single`; one holding **>= 60%** is `lead`; anything flatter is `shared` with **`ownerId: null`**. This is the Meera case Ali flagged: `Autonomous` holds four todos that are four different people's builds, and the old "most todos wins" rule made her owner of the whole list on the strength of holding two. Shared is a description, not a defect, so it does not raise a risk flag; only genuinely unassigned work still does. Staff are excluded from ownership entirely, so Sohail's 4 todos never make him owner of Meghana's build. **(3) Per-person attribution.** `computePerson` now derives every person-level figure from **their own tasks** rather than from whole lists they sit on, via a new `holdings[]` array (per list: their task count, done count, percentage, overdue count, and whether their tasks effectively are the list). Assignee matching moved from **name to id**, so two interns sharing a display name cannot be credited with each other's closures. In the UI, drilling into a project **from a person** now scopes the task list, the update threads and the open questions to that person, with a banner stating exactly what is hidden and a "Show the whole list" toggle; the coverage matrix cell, the person's project cards and their headline percentage all follow the same rule. Harpreet, who holds 1 todo on a 4-todo list, now reads `0/1` rather than inheriting the list's 50%. **(4) The message.** New `backend/src/scripts/lib/internDeliveryCoachNote.js` composes the Basecamp comment Ali posts on each list. It is **deterministic, not LLM-written**, which is a deliberate departure from the narrative layer one file away: the note commits a named person to a completion percentage and a projected finish date, and "cannot invent a number" is a stronger guarantee than "instructed not to". Each note carries what part they are on (`R1 - Data`, release 2 of 3, and what finishing it is worth against the whole build), the KPIs (closed last 7 days vs the 7 before, updates posted, days quiet, past-due count, and the portfolio benchmark for context), the projected finish as a real date, and a tone branch on status from recognition through to a direct read when work has stalled, closing with a named standard and a reply-by-Friday ask. Two notes are generated per project: one to the list, and one per holder scoped to their own tasks. Rendered below "Read of the room" with a **Copy message** button (rich `text/html` plus `text/plain` via `ClipboardItem`, degrading to `writeText` and then to a `textarea` + `execCommand`, because this report is usually read as a `file://` attachment out of Outlook) and an **Open the list in Basecamp** link.

  - Notes: **Three defects were found by looking at the screenshots, not by the tests.** (a) The "Read of the room" panel said `Oct 6, 2026` while the message directly beneath it said `October 7, 2026` about the same forecast: the client parsed date-only strings as UTC midnight and rendered them in local time, so every due date, start date and projected finish displayed a day early for any reader west of Greenwich.

Eleven campaigns · none has ever run

The campaigns, and when each one is used

You never pick a campaign by hand. The system picks one per person per run, by sorting the proposals. This is what each one is for and what has to be true about a person before it can win.

A correction to what I told you yesterday. I said there were sixteen campaigns and that the worked example's winner was explorer_high_intent_fast_track at tier 3. Both were wrong. There are eleven registered campaigns; that key exists only in a test fixture, not in the system; and the real high-intent campaign is explorer_enrollment_ready at tier 5. My check had counted a test file as proof. The worked example is corrected.

How to read the tier

Lower number wins. The tier is the campaign's claim on a person's attention, and only one campaign can win per person per run.

TierMeaning
1–2Something is wrong. Stop selling and fix it.
5–6They are showing real buying signals, or they have stalled and are losable.
7–8Normal progress. Teach them the next thing.
9–10Keep in touch. Lowest claim; loses to everything.

The learner campaigns — CPN and Colaberry Training

Eight campaigns, proposed by four generators. Registered to colaberry / colaberry-training, purpose "Explorer learner nurture".

2explorer_friction_recovery proposed by: friction recovery

Something is blocking them and they are stuck on it. This is the highest-priority campaign in the learner set, because a person fighting a broken thing should never receive a sales email about the next cohort.

Wins when
friction is detected on the account
Use it for
unblocking — a login problem, a payment failure, a lesson that will not load
Note
if the friction needs a person rather than an email, the same generator proposes a human task at the same tier instead
5explorer_enrollment_ready proposed by: high intent

They are showing real buying signals and nothing is in their way. The closest thing in the set to a sales message, and it is deliberately fenced by four conditions that must all hold.

Wins when
the HIGH_INTENT overlay is present and their intent score clears the threshold and their friction score is under 25 and email is eligible for them
Score
their intent score, capped at 100 — so the keenest person wins a tie
Use it for
someone asking about dates, prices or how to start
Note
the friction test is the interesting one: high intent and a problem means the problem wins at tier 2
6explorer_activation_never_started proposed by: activation rescue

They signed up and never began. A different problem from stalling part-way, and it gets its own campaign rather than a shared one, because the message is genuinely different.

Wins when
state is NEW_EXPLORER or ACTIVATING, and they have never engaged
Use it for
a first-step nudge — "here is lesson one"
Channel
email if eligible, otherwise an in-app nudge
6explorer_activation_restart proposed by: activation rescue

They started and stopped. Same tier as never-started, same generator, different campaign — because "come back to lesson 3" and "start lesson 1" are not the same email.

Wins when
state is NEW_EXPLORER or ACTIVATING and they have engaged before
Use it for
re-entry — picking up where they left off
Note
these two share a tier, so the score decides between them, and the generator scores them differently on purpose
7explorer_next_lesson proposed by: nurture

They are learning and moving. The ordinary healthy case: keep them going.

Wins when
they are in a learning state and email is eligible
Use it for
the next lesson recommendation
Note
loses to anything urgent, which is the point — a stalled learner should not get a cheerful "next lesson" email
8explorer_community_digest proposed by: nurture

They have joined the community. A narrower digest for people who have actually connected, rather than the general one.

Wins when
state is exactly CONNECTED_TO_COMMUNITY and email is eligible
Use it for
what is happening among their peers
9explorer_weekly_digest proposed by: nurture

The fallback. If nothing more specific applies and they are contactable, they get the weekly digest. Second-lowest claim in the whole set.

Wins when
nothing above applies and email is eligible
Use it for
staying present without a reason to push
Note
this is the one that loses most often, and that is correct — it is the default, not a strategy
10explorer_referral_invite proposed by: nurture

Ask them to bring someone. Lowest claim of all eleven, because asking a favour should never pre-empt helping them.

Wins when
the REFERRAL_READY overlay is present and email is eligible
Use it for
a referral ask, once they are clearly happy

The business campaigns — Colaberry Business and AI Flotation

A different generator with its own tiers. Two registered flows, one per brand, and never crossed: the lookup refuses to return another brand's flow.

5gj_colaberry_business_discovery_questions proposed by: discovery questions

Ask them what they actually need, before proposing anything. Registered to colaberry / colaberry-enterprise, purpose "Layer 2 discovery questions".

Wins when
the brand's flow is approved and the lead is at the discovery stage
Use it for
a business lead whose requirement is not yet understood
Note
until the flow is approved, the proposal is recorded as the gap no_approved_flow:discovery_questions rather than silently skipped
5gj_ai_flotation_discovery_questions proposed by: discovery questions

The same thing for AI Flotation, as a separate campaign. Registered to ai-flotation / ai-flotation. Two brands, two campaigns, deliberately — so one brand's questions can never be sent under the other's name.

Wins when
as above, for an AI Flotation lead
Use it for
consulting, automation and app-build enquiries
Never for
business training — AI Flotation does not sell it, and the request is referred to Colaberry Business instead

The business generator also proposes things that are not campaigns. At tier 6 a clarification_question when a lead has stalled; at tier 7 capability_education or a case_study; at tier 8 another clarification; at tier 9 a nudge; and at tier 1 — above everything — a suppression, when the right answer is to stop contacting them.

Those are content purposes rather than registered campaign keys, which is why they are not in the list of eleven. They still need approved content to execute.

REVali_personal_outreach ownership: shared

This one is yours, and the system can only ever prepare it. Registered as shared rather than journey, because the existing hourly cron enrols people into it with no journey receipt at all.

Mode

Can be
paused from the Controls tab
Cannot be

Today

The shared ownership has a practical consequence: a send-time hold that cannot read a receipt leaves ordinary Ali-outreach sends exactly as they are today, and holds only a send whose receipt it actually found. Journey-owned campaigns fail closed instead.

When no campaign may run at all

Checked before any proposal is even considered. Any one of these and the person is left alone entirely.

ReasonMeans
convertedthey already bought. Stop selling
unsubscribedthey opted out
dncdo not contact
consent_revokedconsent withdrawn
kill_switchthe global stop is on — or the health record could not be read at all, which is treated the same way on purpose
campaign_inactivethe campaign itself is switched off

What this means in practice

You do not choose campaigns. You choose which ones exist and are approved. After that the sort decides, the same way every time, and records why.

So the lever you actually hold is the content: which of these eleven have approved assets for a given brand and offer. Today none of the learner brands has a single approved content rule, which is why the whole pipeline would stop at step 6 even with every switch on.

If you want to…Do this
Stop one campaign reaching anyonepause its channel, or set the campaign inactive
Change what a campaign saysedit the campaign's sequence — the journey reads it, it does not own it
Make a campaign usable at allapprove a content rule for its brand and offer family
Stop a campaign winning so oftenyou cannot tune the tiers from the UI — they are in code, by design, so the ordering stays reproducible
See which campaign won and whythe Decisions tab: the winner, every loser, and each loser's reason

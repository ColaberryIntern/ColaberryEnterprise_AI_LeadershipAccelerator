Growth Journey OS — how to use it

It decides what should happen next for one person in one brand's journey, records that decision, and — only when you explicitly allow it — acts on it. Right now it is watching nothing and contacting nobody. This page is how you look at it, switch it on a notch at a time, and switch it off again.

4Switch it on, a notch at a time

Nine steps. Steps 1–8 contact nobody. Only step 9 can reach a person, and it is a separate decision.

  1. Seed tenant memberships hard to undo Grants journey access to the admins who need it.
    The cost: the first membership row closes the tenancy fallback for every admin at once. Any admin without a row loses legacy cross-tenant reads too. This is the only step on this list that is not cleanly reversible, so decide who is on the roster before running it. The script refuses unless you acknowledge the exact lock-out count.
  2. Turn the master flag on reversible GROWTH_JOURNEY_ENABLED=true, then restart the backend.
    Routes start answering and the workspace shows real configuration. Still decides nothing. Unset it and restart to undo.
  3. Do the configuration that actually matters reversible Approve one content rule per learner brand; write queue_capacity and queue_assignee rows for sales and solution_architect; move the programmes off draft.
    This is the step that makes the screens stop being empty. Without it a decision cannot resolve no matter which flags are on. Set the rows back to paused to undo.
  4. Let it watch reversible GROWTH_JOURNEY_SIGNAL_INGEST_ENABLED, then GROWTH_JOURNEY_CLASSIFICATION_ENABLED.
    It starts reading people and classifying them by brand and path. Acts on nothing.
  5. Let it think reversible Enable GrowthJourneyShadowDecisions in Admin → Agents, then GROWTH_JOURNEY_DECISIONS_ENABLED=true.
    This is the real demonstration. Every night it records what it would do for each person, with its reasoning, and contacts nobody. Turn the agent off to undo — the registry toggle, never a redeploy.
  6. Let it ask for a human reversible GROWTH_JOURNEY_HANDOFFS_ENABLED=true.
    Real handoff rows and tickets appear in the queue, and your staff can work them. Creating a handoff notifies nobody — they come to the queue.
  7. Register the campaigns reversible growthJourneyExecutionSetup register-campaigns and create-flow-drafts, with --confirm-production --expect-count N.
    Dry run by default. Nothing here activates or approves a campaign.
  8. One scope to review mode reversible A review rollout for one brand × programme × email.
    Actions get prepared and wait for a human. Nothing goes out unapproved. Clear the rollout and the scope drops back to shadow on the next run.
  9. Let it act contacts people GROWTH_JOURNEY_EXECUTION_ENABLED=true.
    The only switch that can cause a person to be contacted. This is a separate decision from everything above it, and nothing about the current state argues for haste. If Ali-outreach is ever in scope it needs section 6A written first.

A shortcut worth knowing. Steps 1–3 together are the smallest set that makes this thing demonstrable. Steps 4–6 are what make it interesting. You can stop after any of them.

5Stop it

Eight levers, smallest blast radius first. Reach for the smallest one that covers the problem.

#LeverScopeReaches work already running?

2Clear a rolloutone scope, back to watchingNo

4A queue policy paused or re-cappedone queue's assignmentn/a

7GROWTH_JOURNEY_ENABLED=false + restarteverything; all routes 404No
8The global kill switchevery send, system-wideYes, at the send gate

Three things about the kill switch that have caught people:

It does not disable the nightly decisions job — that one is categorised behavioral, not outbound. It keeps recording decisions, which contact nobody. So a quiet kill switch is not a stopped system.

Turning it off re-enables nothing. Campaigns stay paused and agents stay disabled until someone turns them back on by hand.

It does not scope. There is no per-brand kill switch — that is what pauses are for, and a pause with no scope at all is refused precisely because it would be a second global switch that could disagree with this one.

Turning a background job on or off is the registry toggle plus the flags — never a redeploy.

6When your staff work the queue

Only relevant after step 6. Three moves, and the choice of disposition matters more than it looks.

DispositionMeansWhat happens next
qualifiedinterested and a fitheld with that person 30 days
convertedthey bought / enrolledclosed
not readyreal interest, wrong timereturned to the system after 14 days
nurturekeep them warmreturned to the system after 14 days
no contactdo not contact againclosed, no further automated contact
disqualifiednot a fitclosed

"not ready" and "nurture" hand the person back to the machine. The other four close them out. Choosing "nurture" for someone who asked not to be contacted puts them back in the queue — pick on that basis, not on which word sounds softest. This is in the staff guide, and it is the one thing worth saying twice.

8Decisions only you can make

None of these blocks anything today. All of them will matter before step 9.

Section 6A — the Ali-outreach policy

The policy does not exist: what may be prepared in your name, by whom, on what evidence. Until it is written, three independent mechanisms keep it inert — nothing generates it, the channel answers review whatever a rollout says, and it cannot be rolled out at all. It can be paused from the Controls tab and cannot be started from it.

When you write it, the shape it has to take: a lead may merit your outreach on an explicit request inside 72 hours or a proposal-stage pipeline position — and in both cases it stays review-only forever, with the daily cap honoured. "Review-only forever" is what the channel is, not a transitional setting.

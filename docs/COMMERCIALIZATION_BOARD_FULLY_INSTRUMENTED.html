Commercialization Command Center, running with everything turned on

This is /admin/commercialization as it would read once the instrumentation behind it is finished and rows are actually flowing. The layout, the wording, the five-state vocabulary and the tile order are taken from the real page's source. Only the numbers are invented, and they are marked like this wherever they appear.

Why the numbers are marked. The whole reason this page exists is that an unrecorded metric rendered as a number is worse than no number at all. A mockup of that page that quietly invented figures would be the exact failure it was built to prevent. So every simulated value is underlined, the banner stays on screen while you scroll, and nothing here may be quoted outside this document.

1. What this page actually is

It is a board that answers one question: what can we prove about the commercial business right now, and what can we not yet measure? It is deliberately not a sales dashboard. A sales dashboard assumes the numbers exist and argues about what they mean. This one starts a step earlier, because for most of these ten metrics nothing was ever recorded.

The rule it enforces, at render time rather than by convention: a tile shows a number only if a number was measured. An unmeasurable tile prints no digits at all — not a dash, not a placeholder, because any glyph in the value slot gets read as a value. That is why the board has five states and not two.

StateShows a number?What it means, and the decision it should drive
● MeasuredYes A source exists, holds rows, and means what the tile says. Act on it.
◐ Proxy onlyYes, with a warning A near neighbor exists but is not the metric; it over- or under-counts. Quote it only with the caveat attached.
○ Measured zeroYes — "0" The right table exists and is empty. The answer genuinely is zero. This is a business fact, not a gap.
— UndefinedNo A rate over an empty denominator. Showing 0% would claim we lose every deal; we have closed none. Wait.
✕ Not yet measurableNo Nothing of the right shape exists anywhere. This is a build item, not a business result.
The distinction that does the work is between ○ Measured zero and ✕ Not yet measurable. "We have a pipeline of $0" and "we do not record pipeline" look identical on an ordinary dashboard and lead to opposite decisions: one says sell harder, the other says go build the pipe. Today four tiles are the second kind.

4. The funnel underneath it

The tiles are the headline. The funnel is where you find out why a tile moved. There are ten stages, each fired by a named table, column and value — never by a judgement call. That is what makes two people count the pipeline the same way.

The decision worth remembering: an email open is not engagement, and neither is a click. Opens fire from tracking pixels that Apple Mail and corporate pre-fetchers load with no human involved, and links get followed by security scanners. Counting them would report 19,729 engaged where 47 people actually acted. Engaged means replied or answered.

5. Revenue hypotheses — the part you personally operate

Five bets about where revenue comes from. Each carries a dated kill condition, because a hypothesis with a success target and no failure tripwire never dies — it just stays untested until nobody remembers it was a bet. Worst first, so a dying one cannot be scrolled past.

6. How you would actually manage it

This is the part a screenshot cannot show. The board is not something you read; it is something you operate on a rhythm.

  1. Read the status strip before the numbers. The chip counts at the top are the health of the instrument. If a tile that was Measured last week is now Proxy or Not yet measurable, something in the pipeline broke and every number below it is suspect. Instrument first, business second — in that order, every time.
  2. Then read exactly three things. Qualified pipeline (is there future revenue), stage 4 of the funnel (is the top filling), and the worst hypothesis row (is a bet dying). Everything else on the page is supporting evidence for those three.
  3. Treat a measured zero as news, not noise. Zero partner conversations for 41 days is a finding. It is only visible because the pipe works — before the instrumentation it would have been indistinguishable from "we don't track that".
  4. Work the hypothesis panel at the tripwire dates, not continuously. The dates are already set: 30 Nov, 31 Dec, 31 Jan, 28 Feb, 31 Mar, 30 Jun. On each of those, one question per row — did it survive? A kill is yours to make; the board will not make it for you, and it will not let you forget.
  5. When a tile and your gut disagree, check the tile's source line first. Every tile names its table. The two most common causes of a surprising number are a stage firing on the wrong trigger and somebody not logging an event, and both are visible from the source line in about a minute.
  6. Expect the weekly report in your inbox rather than remembering to open this page. weeklyCommercialReport.ts already exists and sends the same figures with the same provenance; the board is where you go when the report raises a question.
The failure mode to watch for once everything is green. The danger is not that the numbers are wrong. It is that people stop reading the provenance line and start quoting the tile. Every external figure still has to come from the Claim Registry: no row, no claim. A measured tile is permission to look up the row, not a substitute for it.

The shape of the work. Nine of the ten rows above name the same ticket, and most of them need a write rather than a table. Two of the tables these tiles need already exist and have never been written to. So the instrumentation is largely built and unused — which is better news than it sounds, because it means the gap is operational discipline, not engineering months.
The one thing the board cannot fix. Every stage fires on a recorded event. If nobody logs the demo, the demo did not happen as far as the board is concerned — and it will say ○ Measured zero with total confidence. Full instrumentation moves the risk from "we cannot see" to "we did not log", and the second one is a habit, not a feature.

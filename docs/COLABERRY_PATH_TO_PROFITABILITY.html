1. The finding that changes the question

Account "4300 ISA Placement Income" is not an ISA placement book. It carries $151,841 over eight months. $17,211 of that — 11.3% — is traceable placement-fee cash, from four named students. The rest looks like legacy $250-a-month training subscriptions booked into an account named after something else.

Everything else settling through PaySimple in 2026 is $100, $149, $181.82, $199, $250 and $400 recurring training-subscription and membership drafts, plus $500 and $3,000 enrollment down payments. Those are tuition.

Why the name misleads, precisely. The workbook organizes income by PAYMENT PROCESSOR, not by what was sold. The account is literally "4300 ISA Placement Income (Paysimple)" sitting under a column headed "ISA Placement (Paysimple)", and its sibling is "4200 Training Platform Revenue (Paypal)" under "Tuition & Training (Paypal)". Account 4300 means "cash that arrived through PaySimple". That is all it means.

Real ISA placement income is about $2,150 a month and ends May 2027. It is a line item, not the business.

This matters because the two have opposite risk shapes, and the plan you write is different in each case.

If it were an ISA book

It winds down on a schedule you can forecast. You know the end date, you plan around it, and nothing you do changes the curve. The real ISA run-off does behave this way — and it is nearly over: $7,877 scheduled across 3 contracts, gone by May 2027.

What it actually is

A subscription book that keeps paying indefinitely — some schedules run to 2034 — and can stop all at once, the moment subscribers decide they are no longer receiving anything. It does not expire. It cancels.

The assumption doing all the work: If the program ends and service visibly stops, this book is exposed to mass cancellation and the real decay is far steeper than the end dates imply. No dataset can settle this. It is a service-delivery question, and it is the single largest financial risk in this plan.

5. Do we need to lay people off?

Largely, it has already happened by attrition. Commission cost has fallen 78% since 2023 — from $27,690 a month across 40 to 50 people to $6,175 across 14. Teaching and TA commission reached zero in August with nobody dismissed: the classes ended and the cost went with them.

Do not cut the mentors in isolation. The retainer is $3,500 a month and the book those mentors support pays 4× that every month. Saving $3,500 and triggering a cancellation wave is the most expensive economy available here. The wind-down has to be sequenced against the book, not run independently.

The real decision is invisible in this P&L. $58,787 of commission sits outside the school class, and the health insurance line of $1,519 a month proves W2 employees exist whose wages do not appear here at all. No salaried decision should be taken before that register arrives.

9. Survive, and thrive

Survive

Shape. Protect the legacy book, correct the leaks, teach out cleanly, hold the cost base flat and let membership grow without new investment.

Requires. No new hiring. The retention offer landing before the sunset news. The November cohort delivered.

Outcome. A smaller business that is still here, funded by a book that keeps paying because subscribers still receive something.

Risk. The book is one badly-worded announcement away from a cancellation wave.

Thrive

Shape. Everything in Survive, plus one institutional reference customer in the first half of 2027 and a seller funded on the back of it.

Requires. Deal one. A paywall, so there is something to sell. The discipline to keep taught delivery under 15 percent of volume.

Outcome. 10,000 learners becomes arithmetic rather than ambition.

Risk. Deal one is a single point of failure and nothing substitutes for it.

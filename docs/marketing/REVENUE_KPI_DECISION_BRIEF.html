Revenue reporting: what we have, and whether to build the tab

Answers three questions: what revenue figures exist today, what KPIs are registered, and whether to mask them here or build a separate Revenue report.

The short answer

We have recurring-revenue numbers, and they are real. /admin/revenue reports MRR $6,765, ARR $81,180, 35 active subscribers and ARPU $193.29, computed from paid enrolments. The Revenue report already exists — it is simply not the Marketing › Performance tab.

What is missing is the cash view, not the revenue view. MRR is what members are contracted to pay. Net revenue — what actually landed, net of refunds — still has no source wired, which is why the marketing surface correctly reads “Unavailable”. Both statements are true at once.

But MRR is overstated by roughly 26%. Lapsed members are never removed from it — see section 2. Fix that before anyone quotes $81,180 externally.

2. Two defects in that page

MRR counts members who stopped paying — roughly 26% overstated

In subscriptionAnalyticsService.ts, MRR is accumulated for every annual/monthly member (kpis.mrr += monthlyAmount, line 332) with no exclusion for lapsed. Lapse is detected further down the same loop (line ~352) and only ever pushed into the attention array — it never decrements MRR or the subscriber count.

The page itself lists 9 lapsed monthly members, overdue between 2 and 61 days. At $199 each that is ~$1,791/mo still inside the $6,765 — about 26% of MRR, and roughly $21,500 of the $81,180 ARR.

The service header says so plainly: lapse is “COMPUTED, not stored — nothing in this codebase flags them today… it does not revoke access.” So this is a known shape, not a hidden bug — but the KPI tile does not carry the caveat, and $81,180 is the number that would get quoted.

Subscriber tenure shows 114% and 213% “carry over”

The panel is subtitled “how many carry over from the bucket above”, which describes retention — and retention above 100% is impossible. The service is honest about what the number really is: “a snapshot ratio (this bucket’s headcount vs. the bucket above it, right now) — not per-person cohort tracking across time.”

Comparing today’s Month 3 bucket to today’s Month 2 bucket compares different cohorts, so the ratio is not carry-over at all. The first step compounds it: Month 1’s 1% is measured against 740 Explorers, a different population from every bucket after it. The fix is a label change, not a calculation change — the code is right and the caption is wrong.

5. Mask, or build a separate report?

Neither, in that order of priority:

First — fix the invalid metric, not the layout

Four figures on the tab render as ordinary trustworthy numbers off a metric the registry marks invalid. marketing.campaign_visitors takes GREATEST(site_visitors, email_unique_clickers) — the larger of two different populations — and reports it as one visitor count. The two sets overlap by an unknown amount, so the true number lies between the max and the sum, and the query returns one endpoint.

It then feeds High Intent %, Visitor→Lead %, Conversion Rate and Total Visitors, and it is deliberately excluded from the tab’s “cannot be computed” notice. So it is the one number on the page that is wrong and looks right — strictly worse than the “Unavailable” cards, which are honest.

Correcting the denominator moves numbers people have read for months. That is a decision to make deliberately, which is why it is still open as docs/marketing/ESCALATION-002-fabricated-metrics.md.

Second — group the dead cards, do not hide them

Masking makes the gap invisible, and an invisible gap gets rediscovered in three months as a fresh surprise. The registry’s own rule is that unavailable and invalid are not zero and should render as themselves. The presentation fix is to collect the money cards into one labelled “Not yet measurable” block instead of interleaving them with live figures, so four dead tiles stop diluting the trustworthy ones. Cheap, needs no new data.

Third — the separate report already exists; link to it rather than rebuild it

/admin/revenue is the Revenue report. The marketing tab should not grow a second one, and the separation is right for a real reason rather than tidiness: subscription revenue is member-grain, marketing is visitor- and campaign-grain. Putting both under one heading called “Revenue” is exactly what invited engagement counts to be read as money in the first place — which is why that tab is now “Campaign performance”.

The cheap, high-value change is a pointer: where the campaign tab says revenue cannot be computed, say where revenue does live. Right now someone who wants the number has no way to learn from that page that the number exists one nav item away.

7. Found while verifying the above

A $4,500 default sits on a live payment path

paysimpleService.ts:399 — const amount = params.amount || 4500; in createEnrollmentInvoice(). Its only production caller, enrollmentController.ts:62, does not pass an amount, so the default is what applies. The router is mounted (server.ts:228) and the mode follows env.paysimpleEnv, so under a live key this mints a real $4,500 hosted payment link.

Two other services already carry comments recording that 4500 is not the price and that the offer is a monthly subscription — campaignLinkService.ts and marketingAnalyticsService.ts were both corrected. This caller was not.

Downgraded after checking — it has never fired. revenueDashboardService’s header records the measurement that settles it: SELECT COUNT(*) FROM enrollments WHERE amount_paid = 4500 returns 0. Nobody has ever been charged it. So this is a dormant landmine on a path nothing currently uses, not a live incident.

Still worth removing — a money default that is wrong by billing model rather than by amount (a single $4,500 charge standing in for a monthly subscription) is the kind of thing that fires the day someone reuses the helper. But it is a backlog item, not a page.

The same 4500 fiction also still drives two reporting surfaces: opportunityScoringService.ts:18 and strategic-intelligence/scenarioSimulationEngine.ts:25, where it produces projected-revenue and scenario figures from a price that is not the price. governanceService.ts:212 defaults to it too, though that one is at least configurable.

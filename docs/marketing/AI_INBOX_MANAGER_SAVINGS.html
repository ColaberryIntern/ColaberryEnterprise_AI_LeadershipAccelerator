AI Inbox Manager — what it saved

Fifty days of a shared student job-search inbox, 17 September to 5 November 2026.

Thirty students · 20,773 messages processed · built by Kesetebirhan Delele, on a pipeline begun by Jude Ojobo · prepared 5 November 2026

~$4,700 saved, mid estimate
Staff time avoided, less what the system cost to run. The range across the estimate is $2,200–$7,200. It rests on an estimate of hours, not a measurement of them.

What it would have cost by hand, against what it cost to run

US dollars over the fifty days. One measure, one axis. The hatched bar is a calculation; the solid bar is recorded spend.

Estimated — a calculation over hours nobody measured Recorded — taken from the system’s own spend log
The bar on the left is not a measurement. The hours are the builder’s estimate of how long this handling takes by hand; the rate is a single assumed $30/h, the average of the three placeholder rates he supplied, standing in until Colaberry’s real cost replaces it. The spread shown is the spread in the hours, not in the rate. Staff time spent approving notices is not subtracted.

Where the 20,773 messages went

The saving comes from the ratio, not the volume: almost everything is routine, and a small remainder carries a deadline.

Two things this deliberately does not claim. That a student was reached — during this period delivery sat behind two independent keys that default to off, so the 167 counts notices drafted, not students notified. And that the system beats a person — no human error rate for this task was ever measured, and the builder asked that the comparison not be made.

Three decisions that are yours

1. Do we generate narrative?

His product's whole value is that an LLM writes the story from analysed code. Our compiler's stated guarantee is that it invents nothing — that is why a student can defend every line of it at the Expo.

These are in direct tension. The reconciliation available to us and not to him: generate only from verified evidence, and let the mentor approve the text before it publishes. The gate already exists and already works.

2. Is the audience a recruiter?

He built for recruiters explicitly — search, shortlists, consented contact. If ours is for hiring managers a student sends a link to, that is a different and simpler product, and we should say so rather than half-build his.

3. Is intake worth saving?

Of 494 enrollments, 35 have any project, 7 a repo URL, 1 a business problem. Either that flow gets fixed, or the page should be built entirely on the repo — which is what he did.

My recommendation

Do 1 and 2 this week. One removes a live misleading claim; the other unlocks 39 pieces of real student writing that currently cannot reach any page. Neither is large and both make the page more honest immediately.

Then 3 and 4. Deepening the repo read is the highest-value build, because the repo is the only evidence we have that a student actually earned.

Decide 5 explicitly. Generated narrative is the biggest change to what this platform promises. It should be a decision, not a drift.

💡 The change in one sentence

A demo-prep card used to be a title and a box. Now it teaches you to present.

Open any PREP-1…PREP-6 task and you land in a six-stage workspace — 🎓 Learn → 🎯 Prepare → 🛠️ Build → 🎙️ Practice → 🚀 Present → 🔄 Reflect & Share — opening on the stage that matches what that task actually asks for.

Everything that worked before still works: 📥 same hand-in box, same rules, same points. 👩‍🏫 Demo Day is still marked by staff.

🗣️ Say to the class: “You're about to prepare a presentation of your own project. You're not being asked to be a great speaker. You're being asked to explain what you built, to a named audience, in a fixed time, without hiding behind jargon.”
🧭 Stop 1 · opens on the stage your task needs

🎓 Learn — what good and bad actually look like ● LIVE

The lesson for that card's presentation type: 🎯 the objective, 📦 what to hand in, ⏱️ a timed outline, 🔤 vocabulary your audience may not know, 🏋️ a drill, and 📊 the rubric with its weights.

⭐ The part worth pausing on: there's a worked ✅ good example and a worked ❌ bad one, each annotated. The bad one is the teaching. A good example alone only teaches imitation — the counter-example teaches judgement, and judgement is what you're hired for.
🧭 Stop 2 · the question that changes the whole deck

🎯 Prepare — who is this for? ● LIVE

Two questions and a checklist, 💾 saved as you type. The checklist comes from the template you're on — so it's the checklist for that kind of presentation, not a generic one.

🔥 Say to the class: “‘Hiring managers who've never seen this’ and ‘my team’ are two completely different decks. Pick one. 👉 The prompt in the next stage reads this answer, so being vague here costs you twice.”
🧭 Stop 3 · the one they'll use tonight

🛠️ Build — a deck prompt built from their project ● LIVE

📋 Copy or ⬇️ download it, paste into Claude Code in their own project folder. Out comes presentation.html, speaker-notes.md, demo-runbook.md, and a rehearsal checklist with 5 likely audience questions.

⚠️ Point at the warning box: it lists what the prompt could not fill from their project. That's deliberate. Paste a prompt full of gaps without noticing and you get a deck full of invented detail — numbers you can't defend when someone asks “how do you know?”
🙋 Say this out loud

🚧 What is not live yet ◐ NEXT PHASE

🎙️ Practice, 🚀 Present and 🔄 Reflect & Share aren't wired up. They show a plain sentence saying so, and deliberately offer no buttons — a control that looks live and does nothing teaches you the platform is broken.

📆 Booking a practice room, joining your slot from the card, and having your recording attach itself to the task all land next. Until then: book rooms the way you do today, and hand in evidence through the same box as always. ✅

🤝 Why it's worth saying: it's the same honesty the rubric asks of you in a demo. Name the limit precisely instead of implying everything works.

🎯 The ask

✅ What to do before the Expo

  1. 📖 Open your PREP-3 card and read the Learn stage end to end. ~15 minutes.
  2. 🎯 Fill in Prepare — a specific audience, and what you want to happen afterwards.
  3. 📋 Copy the prompt from Build, run it in Claude Code, and look at what it produced.
  4. 🔍 Fix the gaps it flagged. Don't let it invent a number you can't defend.
  5. 🗣️ Say it out loud once, timed, before you show anyone.
